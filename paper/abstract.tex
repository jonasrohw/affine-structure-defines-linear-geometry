Prior work shows that relations expressed more often in pretraining data are more
likely to admit linear decoders in language-model
representations. We find
that corpus
frequency by itself does not determine this geometry, but we
identify another factor,
\emph{semantic structure} (the attributes that subjects and
objects share),
that plays an important role in shaping geometry.
We construct two controlled synthetic
settings, A and B. The transformer in A sees each attribute-defined
relation fact with low frequency. It reaches \ExpOneSemanticHeldout{} recall on subjects absent from relation
training, while an ordinary least-squares (OLS) map fitted on trained subjects
selects the correct held-out object with \ExpOneSemanticProbe{} accuracy. A frequency-matched random
relation remains at chance under the same probe.
The transformer in B
memorizes a random (i.e., no attribute structure) symmetric relation
 $\Rrnd$
sampled ten times
as often as each of ten controls $r_i$ with rich semantic structure.
 $\Rrnd$'s  accuracy is
\ExpTwoRandom{}, yet the best overdetermined affine fit on its learned entity rows explains
only \ExpTwoRandomRsq{} of target variance, compared with a mean per-seed minimum of \ExpTwoStructuredRsq{} for
the $r_i$.  These results give controlled
counterexamples to a frequency-only account and point to the
importance of semantic structure for relation geometry.
Finally, in our analysis of relations in
the LMEnt-1B model, we find only a weak association 
between corpus frequency and
affine-decoder faithfulness, suggesting that
frequency is only one of many factors in
the emergence
of linear relation geometry in both synthetic and natural data.
