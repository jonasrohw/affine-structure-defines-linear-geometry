
\paragraph{Linear Relational Representations.} Relational linearity asks whether a relation-specific linear map can predict an
object representation from a subject representation. \citet{valer2026relational}
introduce a KL-divergence-based probe for this property and evaluate how it
varies across layers, paraphrases, and models. \citet{merullo2025frequency}
connect the strength of linear relational representations to subject--object
co-occurrence frequency during pretraining. Together, these studies establish
where relational linearity appears and which data properties correlate with it;
our focus is instead the computation that produces the observed linear map.

\paragraph{Lookup and Associative-Memory Accounts.}

In a detailed study of athlete--sport recall in Pythia-2.8B,
\citet{nanda2023factfinding} identify a three-stage circuit: early attention
assembles a multi-token entity representation, early MLPs transform it into a
representation from which the attribute is linearly decodable, and later
attention heads move the attribute information to the prediction position. Their
analysis leaves the distributed computation inside the early MLP stack largely
unresolved, motivating a controlled setting in which that computation can be
identified directly.

\citet{nichani2024associative} analyze shallow transformers as combinations of
attention and MLP associative memories. Their results establish near-optimal
storage capacity and show that a model can trade off storage between attention
value matrices and the MLP. \citet{ravfogel2026geometric} develop a complementary
geometric account: subject embeddings encode superpositions of associated
attributes, while a small relation-conditioned MLP selects the requested
attribute through ReLU gating. The latter mechanism can transfer to new factual
associations when the subject embeddings are re-initialized appropriately,
separating relation-level computation from storage of individual facts.

\paragraph{Relational Generalization and Memorization.}

\citet{cheng2026balancing} study how learning systems combine a relational rule
with memorized exceptions. They introduce transitive inference with exceptions,
analyze the role of representation geometry in kernel ridge regression, and
validate the predicted behavior in fine-tuned language models. Their task
formalizes the tension between rule-based generalization and exception handling.
Our setting asks a complementary mechanistic question: how relation-specific
generalization and fact-specific storage emerge and interact in factual recall.
