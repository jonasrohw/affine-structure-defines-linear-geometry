\section{Motivation: frequency does not consistently predict affine decoding}
\label{sec:lment-frequency}

\citet{merullo2025frequency} report that relations with more frequent
subject--object co-occurrences have better linear relational embeddings (LREs).
Writing their OLMo-7B relation-level comparison as
\begin{equation}
  \bar f_r=\frac{1}{|\mathcal D_r|}\sum_{(s,o)\in\mathcal D_r}n(s,o),
  \qquad \operatorname{corr}_r(\log\bar f_r,C_r)\approx0.82,
  \label{eq:merullo-frequency}
\end{equation}
$n(s,o)$ counts co-occurrences and $C_r$ measures how successfully LRE-based
interventions change the predicted object. They also report consistently high
causality scores above model-dependent frequency thresholds. These observations
suggest exposure frequency as an explanation for relational linearity.

We examine its broader applicability in LMEnt-1B, a one-billion-parameter model
pretrained on English Wikipedia \citep{gottesman2025lment}. We evaluate 5,388 facts
across 58 relations using mean-Jacobian affine decoders
$\hat o_r(s)=D(\beta W_r h_s+b_r)$ \citep{hernandez2024linearity}, where $D$
decodes the top-1 token. Our score is first-token faithfulness,
$\phi_r=\Pr[\hat o_r(s)=o_{\mathrm{LM}}(s,r)]$; frequency counts subject-linked
corpus documents containing the gold object label.

Here the relation-level association is weak:
$\operatorname{corr}_r(\log(1+\bar f_r),\phi_r)=0.095$ across the 48 relations
with at least two distinct predicted answer tokens (Figure~\ref{fig:lment-frequency}A).
Within relations, higher frequency can also accompany lower faithfulness.
Comparing upper and lower subject--object frequency quartiles, 11 of 27 eligible
relations decrease, three are unchanged, and 13 increase. Official-language
faithfulness falls from $84.4\%$ to $50.6\%$, whereas country currency rises from
$42.3\%$ to $69.0\%$ (Figure~\ref{fig:lment-frequency}B).

Thus, frequency does not consistently predict affine-decoder faithfulness in
this model. Our metric and corpus counts differ from those underlying
Equation~\ref{eq:merullo-frequency}, so this is evidence against a universal
frequency-only explanation, rather than a refutation of their measured
correlation. This motivates our controlled experiments: can low exposure support
affine geometry, and can high exposure fail to produce it?
Appendix~\ref{app:lment-frequency} gives the evaluation and aggregation details.
