\section{Frequency and affine decoding in a pretrained language model}
\label{sec:lment-frequency}

In pretrained LMEnt-1B-1E, more frequent facts sometimes have lower affine-decoder
faithfulness within the same relation. We aggregate the existing frequency
curves at checkpoint \texttt{step109672}. LMEnt supplies a pretrained language
model and an entity-annotated corpus that lets us connect its predictions to
document counts \citep{gottesman2025lment}. This analysis tests whether the measured
frequency orders decoder faithfulness in the observed data.

\paragraph{Model, data, and decoder.}
The checkpoint uses an 18-block OLMo2 architecture with residual width 2,048.
The factual subset of our triplet dataset contains 60 relation types; 58 have
enough accepted examples for the eight-example LRE protocol. After filtering to
prompts where the model predicts a nonempty prefix of the gold answer, the
evaluation contains 5,388 facts. We use the mean-Jacobian affine linear relational
embedding (LRE) of \citet{hernandez2024linearity}, estimated from eight separate
examples per relation. The map takes the subject's hidden state to a predicted
output state, which the model's output head decodes. Faithfulness is exact
agreement between the LRE's top-1 token and the full model's top-1 token. Overall
faithfulness is $2{,}751/5{,}388=51.1\%$ when weighting facts equally and $54.4\%$
when weighting relations equally. The archived evaluation selects a layer per
relation and a shared scale $\beta=4$ on the reported evaluation set. Its scores
can therefore overestimate performance on a new test set.

\paragraph{Frequency measurements.}
We retain all three axes from the original graphs. Subject frequency counts
distinct corpus documents or chunks linked to the subject's Wikidata identifier.
Subject--object frequency restricts this count to documents containing the gold
object label, matched case-insensitively at word boundaries with the recorded
country and continent demonym aliases. Subject--relation frequency instead
requires one of the recorded lexical cues for the relation. These are corpus
proxies: a co-occurrence need not express the fact, and a cue match need not
connect the subject to the gold object. Zero means that the counting procedure
found no matching document, rather than establishing zero training exposure.

\paragraph{Aggregate results.}
Pooled frequency has little rank association with faithfulness
(Table~\ref{tab:lment-aggregate}). To avoid attributing changes in relation
composition to frequency, we also compare the upper and lower frequency
quartiles within each relation. We keep ties together and require distinct
quartile cutoffs, at least ten facts in each tail, and at least two distinct
model-predicted tokens across the relation. This leaves 27 relations for subject
and subject--object frequency, and 21 for subject--relation frequency.
For subject--object counts, 11 relations have lower faithfulness in the upper
quartile, three have equal scores, and 13 have higher scores. The mean difference
across relations is $-0.4$ percentage points. The corresponding means for subject
and subject--relation counts are $+1.4$ and $-1.5$ points.
Figure~\ref{fig:lment-frequency} shows that these small means combine differences
in both directions.

\paragraph{Examples with opposite frequency trends.}
The official-language relation has $84.4\%$ faithfulness among facts with at most
four subject--object document matches and $50.6\%$ among facts with at least 129
matches (Table~\ref{tab:lment-examples}). Organization headquarters and landmark
country also decline, from $33.3\%$ to $7.1\%$ and from $25.3\%$ to $8.2\%$.
All three have negative quartile differences on the other two frequency axes as
well. Country currency provides a positive comparison: faithfulness rises from
$42.3\%$ to $69.0\%$ with subject--object frequency and increases on both other
axes. We selected these examples to illustrate the different directions; the
aggregate includes every relation meeting the stated criteria.

These observations connect the controlled experiments to a pretrained model:
the measured frequency does not consistently order affine-decoder performance.
They do not establish that frequency has no predictive information, or that a
low-scoring relation lacks every possible affine representation. The metric
tests a particular mean-Jacobian decoder and only the first answer token.
Answer-token distributions, prompt choice, and filtering can affect its score.
Appendix~\ref{app:lment-frequency} reports the aggregation details and checks for
constant answer tokens and small frequency groups.
